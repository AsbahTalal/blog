01 / AEROSPACE · MECHANICAL · TEAM PROJECT

DRON: Disaster Response Observation Network

● flight testedairframe designfall 2025 – spring 2026

V4 production drone in SolidWorks: carbon fiber arms and legs

Firefighters at a structural fire need to know where the hotspots are before they go in. DRON is a swarm of small quadcopters that fly around a burning building, map it in 3D with two cameras, and paint thermal data onto the map for first responders. On our team I designed the airframe: the trade studies, CAD, drop-test simulations and the fixes after every broken part.

THE PROBLEM

My chassis had to carry a Raspberry Pi 5, flight controller, GPS, two cameras and a thermal camera, survive a 10 m fall, and keep a 1.5 factor of safety, while staying light enough for 10 minutes of flight.

WHAT BROKE

During motor testing, PLA and polycarbonate arms snapped. One motor was underperforming, so another spun up to compensate and overloaded its arm. I moved the arms to glass-fiber PLA, then to carbon fiber tubes.

DESIGN DECISIONS

  • I ran weighted trade studies for arm material, leg material and chassis shape; carbon fiber won both material studies.
  • I designed the arms and legs around 16 mm OD, 2 mm wall carbon fiber tubes with matching M3 hole patterns, so parts swap.
  • I modeled a Hybrid-X chassis with 84 mm of clear interior for components and wiring.
  • I built a custom 4S 18650 pack for under $25, vs $65+ for an off-the-shelf battery.

RESULTS

  • Sustained flight on hardware in March 2026, holding steady in wind.
  • The leg failed at its intended failure point on a hard landing, protecting the electronics.
  • My FEA drop tests on the legs guided the foot and joint design.
  • ~$430 per drone in parts.

02 / MECHANICAL · ELECTRONICS · TEAM PROJECT

ORIO: 3D-Printed Equatorial Star Tracker

● prototypingfall 2025 – spring 2026

Cycloidal drive in SolidWorks: 21 pins around a 60 mm pitch circle for a 20:1 reduction

Stars drift across the sky at the sidereal rate, about 15 arcseconds every second. Leave a camera still for a long exposure and they smear into trails. ORIO is a 3D-printed equatorial mount that sits on a normal tripod and turns the camera with the sky, so stars stay as points. On our team I designed the drive, the base and the electronics.

REQUIREMENTS

  • Track about the right ascension axis at the sidereal rate.
  • Minimal star drift over a 30 s exposure with a 50 mm lens.
  • Carry at least 2 kg of camera and lens.
  • Mount to standard 1/4"-20 and 3/8" tripod and camera threads.
  • Reduce backlash, and run about 2 hours on battery.

THE GEARING PROBLEM

To hit roughly 0.3–0.5 arcseconds per motor step, the drive needs a total reduction of about 400–720:1. Printed spur gears have too much backlash at that precision, which shows up as streaked stars.

DESIGN DECISIONS

  • I started with a 3D-printed planetary gearbox, then switched to a cycloidal drive after review feedback that cycloidals have near-zero backlash.
  • I designed the cycloidal stage: 21 pins, 60 mm pitch radius, 1.5 mm eccentricity, for 20:1 in one stage.
  • Belt stage from a 1" to a 4.5" pulley multiplies the reduction further.
  • I designed a print-in-place hinged base for manual polar alignment, sized from my own tripod measurements.

ELECTRONICS & CONTROL

  • I wired an ESP32 to drive two NEMA 17 steppers through TMC2209 drivers.
  • 14.8 V 4S LiPo with a 5 A fuse and buck converter; about 1 hour at 3 A.
  • I built the web app (HTML, CSS, JavaScript) for manual setup and automatic sidereal tracking.
  • Next: a GoTo mode that points at any object once calibrated.

03 / RESEARCH · AIR QUALITY · NASA SEES 2024

Green Space, PM2.5 & Respiratory Illness

● presentedsummer 2024 · 5 weekswith co-researcher Mina Chen

Green space as a share of county area: Bay Area counties reach ~20%, LA-area counties stay under ~7%

Fine particulate matter (PM2.5) is small enough to pass through the lungs into the bloodstream, and studies tie it to pneumonia and other respiratory illness. Other studies show parks and water absorb air pollution. Almost no one had connected the two at the county level, so we asked: do counties with more green space have cleaner air and less respiratory illness?

DATA

  • Annual PM2.5 for each county from the U.S. EPA.
  • COPD and asthma cases (ages 40+) and community-acquired pneumonia cases from California's Department of Health Care Access and Information.
  • Park and open-water area from county parks departments, plus yearly county populations.

METHOD

  • 15 counties in two groups, 9 around San Francisco and 6 around Los Angeles, every year from 2016 to 2022.
  • Normalized illness counts by population so big and small counties compare fairly.
  • Grouped counties by green-space share (over 10%, 5–10%, under 5%, under 2.5%) and ranked each on every measure.

WHAT WE SAW

  • The Bay Area group had far more green space, up to ~20% of county area in San Francisco.
  • The counties with under 2.5% green space (Kern, Riverside, San Bernardino) had the highest PM2.5, reaching ~15–22 µg/m³.
  • Respiratory case rates fell in most counties after 2020 even where PM2.5 stayed high, so other factors were clearly at play.

NEXT STEPS WE PLANNED

  • Difference-in-differences analysis in R to test for a causal link, not just a correlation.
  • GIS to measure green space and water area directly instead of relying on county websites.

04 / HARDWARE · SOFTWARE · HACKLAHOMA 2026

PetPulse: AI Pet Health Monitor

● Winner, Best Hardware● Winner, Best Use of DigitalOceanteam of 4

Home page: track your pet's health, behavior and routine

Your pet can't talk, but PetPulse can help you understand. Pet cameras record hours of footage but never tell you what happened. We wanted something that answers the questions owners actually have: did my dog eat, is she sleeping normally, did something stress her out while I was gone?

HOW IT WORKS

  • A 3-axis accelerometer on an Arduino watches for changes in movement.
  • When something changes, it captures a snapshot and about 5 seconds of audio.
  • Gemini reads the photo and sound and returns a structured result: condition (sleep, eating, mood), status and a timestamped summary.
  • A scoring algorithm decides what is worth flagging and sends an alert.

WHAT WE BUILT

  • React + Vite web app: dashboard, calendar, community, camera and settings.
  • Python FastAPI backend with PostgreSQL for pets, media, AI results and vet history.
  • Breed finder from a photo, bark and whine analysis, and pet profiles with a QR code for vets and sitters.
  • Deployed on DigitalOcean, with Firebase Auth and Cloudflare.

WHAT WAS HARD

  • Getting the Arduino talking to a computer over a private network.
  • Telling meaningful movement apart from sensor noise.
  • Streaming from a webcam that doesn't stream on its own, which we routed through Twitch.

WHAT I LEARNED

  • Structured AI output is far more reliable than free-form descriptions.
  • Sensor systems need retries and storage, not just a model.
  • Next up: per-pet baselines to cut false alarms, and YOLOv8 for posture and activity.

06 / DATA SCIENCE · RESEARCH · TECHNOLOGY STUDENT ASSOCIATION

TSA Data Science & Analytics

● 3rd at TSA Nationals● 1st in Oklahoma ×22023 – 2025

2025: Impact of Urban Sprawl & Green Infrastructure

The Data Science & Analytics event asks you to pick a real-world problem, gather public data on it, and make a clear, evidence-backed case in a written report and a research poster. I competed three years in a row, starting at the 2023 National Conference in Louisville.

2025 · URBAN SPRAWL & GREEN INFRASTRUCTURE

  • Question: how do cities spreading outward affect the environment, the economy and social equity?
  • Pulled data from the EPA, National Library of Medicine, University of Michigan's Center for Sustainable Systems and the Federal Reserve.
  • Showed rising land surface temperatures as cities grow, green buildings lasting ~29 years longer than conventional ones, and home prices pulling away from median income.
  • Recommended green zoning, permeable pavement, green roofs and mixed-use, transit-oriented development.

2023–24 · CRIMINALIZATION OF THE UNDERPRIVILEGED

  • Question: how do poverty and race affect who ends up in prison, and what happens after?
  • Pulled data from the Census Bureau, Bureau of Justice Statistics, U.S. Sentencing Commission and Brookings Institution.
  • A full written report for the 2023 National Conference, then a poster for the 2024 state conference.
  • Boys from the poorest 10% of families are 20× more likely to be in prison by their early 30s than those from the richest families.
  • The 50 cities that rely most on fines have about 5× the typical city's share of Black residents (18.9% vs 3.8%).
  • Cities that cut low-level arrests by 50%+ saw 57% fewer police shootings with no rise in reported crime.
  • Argued for policy changes to reduce incarceration tied to debt and poverty.
