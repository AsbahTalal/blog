01 / AEROSPACE · MECHANICAL · TEAM PROJECT

DRON: Disaster Response Observation Network

● flight testedmechanical subteamfall 2025 – spring 2026

V4 production drone in SolidWorks: carbon fiber arms and legs

Firefighters at a structural fire need to know where the hotspots are before they go in. DRON is a swarm of small quadcopters that fly around a burning building, map it in 3D with two cameras, and paint thermal data onto the map for first responders. I worked on the mechanical subteam, designing and iterating on the airframe.

THE PROBLEM

The chassis had to carry a Raspberry Pi 5, flight controller, GPS, two cameras and a thermal camera, survive a 10 m fall, and keep a 1.5 factor of safety, while staying light enough for 10 minutes of flight.

WHAT BROKE

During motor testing, PLA and polycarbonate arms snapped. One motor was underperforming, so another spun up to compensate and overloaded its arm. We moved to glass-fiber PLA, then to carbon fiber tubes.

DESIGN DECISIONS

  • Weighted trade studies for arm material, leg material and chassis shape; carbon fiber won both material studies.
  • Arms and legs from 16 mm OD, 2 mm wall carbon fiber tubes with matching M3 hole patterns, so parts swap.
  • Hybrid-X chassis with 84 mm of clear interior for components and wiring.
  • Custom 4S 18650 pack for under $25, vs $65+ for an off-the-shelf battery.

RESULTS

  • Sustained flight on hardware in March 2026, holding steady in wind.
  • The leg failed at its intended failure point on a hard landing, protecting the electronics.
  • FEA drop tests on the legs guided the foot and joint design.
  • ~$430 per drone in parts.
